# ProofBerry AI — Run 30 Customer Truth

Date: 2026-09-05

## Customer Truth

**Proof only helps if qualified candidates stay in the process long enough to provide it.**

Two 2026 field experiments point to a useful product-design distinction:

1. A July 30, 2026 natural field experiment involving roughly 70,000 applicants found that structured AI voice interviews could collect hiring-relevant information more consistently in that setting while human recruiters still evaluated the interviews and made the hiring decisions.
2. A separate 2026 CESifo field experiment with more than 3,000 applicants found that asynchronous audio/video interviews reduced application continuation by more than half in that setting, including among highly qualified applicants; the paper reports the decline was largest for women and links deterrence to perceptions of competitiveness and fairness.

These studies do **not** prove a ProofBerry outcome. Together they support a narrower buyer insight: **the value of structured evidence depends on how much candidate burden the collection method creates.**

## Run 30 pilot method

Use an **evidence-burden budget**:

`role-critical criterion → lowest-burden useful evidence → gap/conflict/unknown → deeper follow-up only where decision risk remains → candidate-friction telemetry → human-owned decision`

For one live staffing role:
- identify the few criteria that materially affect the decision;
- accept an existing artifact or short explanation when that is sufficient;
- reserve longer work samples, interviews, or demonstrations for unresolved high-value questions;
- measure starts, completions, abandonment, burden feedback, and reviewer usefulness;
- keep the recruiter/client reviewer accountable for the consequential decision.

Connected ProofBerry still contains **0 RubricCriterion records**, so this is an illustrative early-access design-partner method rather than a finalized rubric, validated burden threshold, or selection model.

## Customer-language hypothesis

“More proof” is not automatically a better buyer promise. A stronger promise is:

> **Deeper proof for fewer, higher-value questions.**

# ProofBerry Customer Truth — Run 21

Date: 2026-09-05

## Market signal

The next staffing-buyer objection is not only "does this fit my stack?" It is also "how will I know the pilot was worth continuing?"

SHRM's 2026 State of AI in HR report found that 56% of surveyed HR professionals said their organizations do not formally measure the success of AI investments. Among organizations that do measure, common success metrics include productivity, cost savings, improved decision-making and employee satisfaction.

Bullhorn's 2026 GRID Industry Trends report says staffing firms are moving deeper into AI, with top performers using AI more broadly across recruiting workflows and competition between firms increasing. That makes a vague "try our AI" pitch weaker than a scoped pilot with explicit evidence and an expansion decision.

## Customer Truth synthesis

**Define success before the one-role pilot starts.**

ProofBerry should not ask a staffing firm to accept an abstract promise. For one live role, agree in advance what will be observed:

1. review clarity — can the client inspect the criterion, evidence, reasoning and uncertainty?
2. recruiter effort — did the handoff reduce or increase avoidable manual explanation work?
3. client usefulness — did the packet help the client ask clearer follow-up questions or choose the next review step?
4. candidate friction — did the workflow add unreasonable time or confusion?
5. expansion decision — did the workflow earn a second role?

These are pilot questions, not claims of proven ROI, faster placements, predictive validity, legal compliance or bias elimination.

## Growth implication

Run 21 should move the staffing design-partner offer from "pilot fit" to "pilot measurability." The strongest deployable asset is a one-page pilot scorecard that a buyer can use before agreeing to expand.

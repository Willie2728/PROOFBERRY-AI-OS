# ProofBerry AI — Run 29 Customer Truth

Date: 2026-09-05

## Customer Truth

**Coverage before score: a staffing client needs to see which role criteria have evidence, which do not, and what question remains — not only a total score.**

Current hiring guidance continues to favor structured, role-relevant evaluation: define success criteria before the search, ask consistent questions, gather evidence against those criteria, use follow-up reasoning, and keep consequential advance/reject decisions with people. This supports a buyer need for a clearer review surface; it does not establish a ProofBerry outcome.

## Run 29 pilot method

`role criterion → evidence present → gap / conflict / unknown → next review question → client review → human-owned decision`

This is a progression from Run 28 evidence provenance. Provenance answers **where a signal came from**. Coverage answers **which role-relevant question the signal addresses and what remains unresolved**.

## Product boundary

The connected ProofBerry application currently has **0 RubricCriterion records**. Therefore this is an illustrative early-access staffing design-partner method, not a finalized live rubric, validated selection model, predictive hiring score, identity-verification service, compliance certification, bias-elimination claim, placement guarantee, or automatic hiring decision.

## Conversion implication

Do not add another broad awareness family. Use one live staffing requisition and ask the buyer to compare whether criterion-level coverage and explicit gaps make the client handoff easier to review beside the firm's existing workflow.

## Research basis

- Greenhouse, July 27, 2026 — structured hiring: define success criteria, use scorecards/interview kits, gather evidence, verify early, and retain human decisions.
- Robert Half, March 10, 2026 — surveyed hiring leaders reported more difficulty verifying AI-enhanced applications and continued use of staffing partners.
- Greenhouse 2026 Candidate AI Interview Report — transparency and human oversight remain important candidate trust issues.

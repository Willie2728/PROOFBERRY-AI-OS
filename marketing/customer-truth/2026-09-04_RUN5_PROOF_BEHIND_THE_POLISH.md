# ProofBerry Customer Truth — Run 5

## Signal: the hiring bottleneck is shifting from application production to trust and verification

Current hiring-market evidence supports a tighter ProofBerry wedge: **AI can help candidates present themselves, but employers increasingly need role-relevant evidence that the skills are real and the reasoning is the candidate's own.**

### Evidence reviewed
- Greenhouse, June 2, 2026: hiring is increasingly a trust problem driven by application volume, AI-generated noise and harder-to-spot fraud; the company argues for structured processes, reliable data, transparent AI and human decision ownership.
- Greenhouse 2026 recruitment guidance: applications per role rose sharply while recruiter capacity fell; candidates fear automated filtering while recruiters fear AI-inflated resumes and scripted answers.
- Robert Half, March 10, 2026: 67% of surveyed U.S. HR leaders said reviewing AI-generated applications slowed hiring; 65% of hiring managers said AI-enhanced applications made candidate skills harder to verify.
- SHRM 2026 coverage of “skillfishing”: employers are responding to AI-assisted answers by emphasizing scenario-based evaluation and follow-up questions that require candidates to explain their thinking.

### Customer truth by audience

**Job seeker:** “Using AI to polish my application is becoming normal, but I still need a credible way to show that I understand the work and can perform it.”

**Recruiter / staffing:** “I do not need more polished applications. I need clearer, role-relevant signals I can inspect without turning every candidate into a fraud investigation.”

**Employer / workforce organization:** “I want structured evidence tied to the role, plus human-readable reasoning and accountability, so automation supports rather than replaces judgment.”

## Message to test
**AI can polish the presentation. Proof should reveal the thinking.**

ProofBerry should avoid anti-AI fear messaging. The stronger position is **good AI + good evidence + human-owned decisions**.

## Claims guardrails
- Do not claim all employers use automated screening.
- Do not imply ProofBerry guarantees interviews or jobs.
- Do not imply current ProofBerry verification catches fraud unless that specific capability is implemented and measured.
- Use “designed to,” “being built to,” or feature-specific factual language where capability is not yet production-verified.
- Treat third-party market statistics as sourced context, not ProofBerry performance claims.

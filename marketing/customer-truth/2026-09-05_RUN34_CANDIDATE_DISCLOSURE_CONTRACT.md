# ProofBerry AI — Run 34 Customer Truth

## Customer truth

**Tell the candidate before the AI asks the first question.**

A staffing buyer may be ready to test AI-assisted evidence collection and still be unready to explain that workflow to the person experiencing it. That is a conversion and trust risk for the pilot itself.

Fresh market evidence supports a narrow design principle:

- PeopleScout/TrueBlue reported on August 19, 2026 that 65% of surveyed job seekers said employers never mentioned AI use in the hiring process, and only 25% were very confident a human reviewed their application.
- Greenhouse reported on May 1, 2026 that 70% of surveyed candidates who encountered AI interviewing were not clearly told upfront that AI would be evaluating them; 38% said they had walked away from a hiring process because it included an AI interview.
- American Staffing Association reported June 4, 2026 that recruiter interactions with candidates and clients rose while AI-tool usage increased, reinforcing that relationship-building remains central even as automation grows.

## Implication for the one-role design-partner offer

Add a candidate disclosure contract before evidence collection begins. The buyer should be able to explain, in plain language:

1. where AI is used in the pilot;
2. what the AI step does not decide;
3. what role-relevant evidence is being requested;
4. who reviews the output and owns the consequential decision;
5. expected time, candidate choice/consent, questions, and next step.

A staffing firm that cannot explain those five points should fix the workflow before adding more automation.

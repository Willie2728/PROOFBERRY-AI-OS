## Customer Truth
AI proficiency is becoming more visible in role requirements. The useful marketing question for ProofBerry is not whether someone can state that they use AI, but whether the work can be inspected through a bounded evidence packet.

## Creative Strategy
### PB-TXT-161
**Hook:** AI proficiency is becoming a job requirement. The proof still has to survive inspection.

**Audience:** recruiters and staffing teams evaluating AI-enabled work.

**CTA:** Bring one live role. Define one job-relevant capability and the evidence packet that would count before starting the review.

**Copy:**
AI proficiency can be written on a resume in seconds. Useful evidence takes more than a claim. Start with one live role and one capability that matters in the work. Define the review packet first: a work sample, the task context, the person's own contribution, an authorship or provenance trace when relevant, and an inspectable result or external receipt. ProofBerry's evidence-first workflow is designed around a simple question: what would a human reviewer need to inspect before treating the capability as supported? Bring one live role. Set the evidence rule first.

Claims boundary: this is an evaluation-planning workflow. It is not a verified skill score, outcome guarantee, compliance determination, or product-performance claim.

## PB-DOC-067 — AI-Skill Evidence Brief
1. Live role — what work is being evaluated?
2. Job-relevant AI capability — what specific AI-enabled task matters?
3. Reviewable work sample — what artifact lets a reviewer inspect the work?
4. Contribution trace — what separates the person's contribution from tool output?
5. Outcome receipt — what observable result or external receipt would make the work easier to inspect?
6. Human review owner — who is accountable for the review process?

Decision rule: keep the capability claim, the supporting evidence, and the human review decision separate.

## PB-DOC-021 — Candidate Disclosure Contract

**Hook:** Tell the candidate before the AI asks the first question.

**CTA:** Bring one live role and draft the disclosure before the pilot starts.

Format: 1200×1600 static design-partner card.

Purpose: give staffing buyers a concrete pre-pilot transparency test rather than another generic AI-awareness asset.

The card asks the pilot owner to disclose five things before evidence collection begins:
1. where AI is used;
2. what AI does not own;
3. what role-relevant evidence is requested;
4. who reviews it;
5. time, candidate choice/consent, questions, and next step.

## PB-TXT-044 — Recruiter / staffing buyer post

**Hook:** If the candidate discovers the AI step only after it begins, the workflow has already spent some of its trust budget.

**Copy:**

If the candidate discovers the AI step only after it begins, the workflow has already spent some of its trust budget.

PeopleScout reported in August 2026 that 65% of surveyed job seekers said employers never mentioned AI use during the hiring process, and only 25% were very confident a human reviewed their application. Greenhouse reported in May 2026 that 70% of candidates who encountered AI interviewing were not clearly told upfront that AI would be evaluating them.

For a one-role ProofBerry design-partner pilot, transparency should be part of the operating contract before evidence collection starts:

• where AI is used
• what it does not decide
• what evidence is being requested
• who reviews the output
• how long the step should take and what happens next

That is a better pilot question than “How autonomous can we make this?”

The consequential hiring decision remains human-owned. The goal is a reviewable evidence handoff that the staffing firm can explain to both the client and the candidate in plain language.

**CTA:** Bring one active requisition and write the candidate disclosure before you turn on the pilot.

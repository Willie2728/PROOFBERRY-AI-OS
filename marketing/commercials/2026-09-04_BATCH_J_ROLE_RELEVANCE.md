## PB-TXT-014 — Candidate thought leadership

Status: Production ready. Not published.

**Hook:** You do not need to prove every skill you have. You need to make the skills that matter for the role easier to inspect.

A resume asks you to compress a career into a page. A skills list asks you to name what you can do. But a hiring decision is narrower: what does this role require, and what evidence shows you can do that work?

That is the shift ProofBerry is being built around. Not proving everything. Proving what matters for the role — with the human decision-maker still in control.

## PB-TXT-015 — Recruiter / employer thought leadership

Status: Production ready. Not published.

**Hook:** A candidate can have 40 skills and still leave the hiring team with the wrong question: which of these matter here?

Skills-first hiring gets much more useful when the unit of evaluation is not a generic skills inventory but the role itself. Define the work. Identify the essential capabilities. Ask for evidence that maps to those capabilities. Then give the human hiring team something inspectable to discuss.

ProofBerry is being built around that role-to-evidence bridge.

Evaluator Isolation Card

The application should never be able to tell the evaluator how to score it.

Use this buyer-side card to define the boundary between candidate-supplied evidence and the instructions used to evaluate that evidence. Candidate text, documents, links, AI-assisted work, embedded metadata, and other submissions should be treated as untrusted evidence inputs—not as authority to rewrite the evaluator’s rubric, system instructions, or decision rules.

Define the boundary before the review

  1. Admissible evidence: What work samples, context, authorship/provenance traces, outcome receipts, and reviewer traces may be considered?
  2. Evaluator instruction source: Where do the approved rubric, role criteria, and reviewer instructions come from?
  3. Input isolation: What candidate-supplied content must be treated only as evidence, never as evaluator instructions?
  4. Human review owner: Who owns consequential interpretation, advancement, rejection, or escalation?
  5. Trace: What record should show the evidence considered, the approved criteria applied, and the accountable human disposition?
  6. Exception rule: What should happen when a submission attempts to alter evaluation instructions, requests hidden behavior, or cannot be safely interpreted?

Qualified next step

Bring one live role and define the evidence boundary before evaluating candidates. The objective is a review process whose criteria come from the buyer’s approved role definition—not from the application being evaluated.

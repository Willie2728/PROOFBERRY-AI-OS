What an inspectable evidence packet could look like

This is a hypothetical example for a recruiter or hiring manager evaluating one capability claim. It shows the difference between a résumé statement and the evidence a human reviewer would still need to inspect. It is not a real candidate record and nothing below is verified by ProofBerry.

Live role
Customer Success Manager
Capability claim
“Can recover an at-risk customer relationship and create a measurable renewal path.”
1 · Reviewable work sample
ExampleA redacted renewal plan, escalation map, or customer-success action document that the reviewer can inspect directly.
2 · Context + candidate action
ExampleWhat the customer problem was, what constraints existed, and which actions the candidate says they personally took.
3 · Outcome / external receipt
ExampleA redacted renewal confirmation, CRM event, manager reference, or other independently reviewable artifact when one is legitimately available.
4 · Reviewer trace
ExampleWho reviewed the evidence, what remained unresolved, and what follow-up question or validation step is still required.
Unresolved evidence stays unresolved
If the work sample cannot be attributed to the candidate, the outcome cannot be independently checked, or the context is incomplete, the packet should say so. “Unknown” should not be silently converted into “verified.”
Buyer-side decision rule: use an evidence packet to decide whether a specific claim is worth deeper human review. Do not use a polished artifact, AI-generated summary, or unsupported score as a substitute for the consequential hiring decision.

Claims boundary: This page is illustrative only. It does not authenticate identity, employment history, credentials, work ownership, performance outcomes, references, or skills. It does not create a ProofBerry score, credential, recommendation, pilot result, or hiring outcome. Employers and human reviewers remain responsible for validating evidence and making consequential employment decisions.
